Odd-Color Amplitude Filters for Lonely Runner Riesz Products

An exact finite cancellation interface, a machine-checked proof, and a precise arithmetic obstruction left for the analytic method.

Jatin Dehmiwal · AI-assisted exploration and formal proof engineering

Abstract

Riesz-product arguments for the Lonely Runner Conjecture gain from selected positive Fourier coefficients but pay for low-order additive relations. We encode the supports of those relations as a hypergraph and introduce a three-atom correlated sign law. On every even support, its mixed moment is zero if and only if the support is odd-colored; when the support is not odd-colored, that moment is exactly one. The law has common coordinate mean \(1/2\), so it retains a constant fraction of first-order gain. Independent sharp scalar filters annihilate the third moment, or the third and fifth moments, separating the treatment of odd and even support sizes. We formalize the exact equivalence, finite algebra, parity step, scalar filters, and positivity of the Riesz factors in Lean 4. The full conjecture is not proved: the remaining problem is to control the odd-color structure of the actual arithmetic relation hypergraph and complete the analytic transfer.

1. Lonely Runner and Riesz products

For distinct positive integers \(V=\{v_1,\ldots,v_n\}\), define

\[\operatorname{ML}(V)=\max_{t\in\mathbb R/\mathbb Z}\min_{v\in V}\|tv\|,\]

where \(\|x\|\) is distance to the nearest integer. The stationary-runner form of the Lonely Runner Conjecture is

Lonely Runner Conjecture
\[\operatorname{ML}(V)\ge\frac1{n+1}.\]

For \(0<\delta<1/2\), let

\[\Phi_\delta(t)=\sum_{v\in V}\mathbf1_{\{\|vt\|\le\delta\}}.\]

If the bad arcs cover the circle, then \(\Phi_\delta\ge1\). Every nonnegative density \(R\) of integral one therefore satisfies

\[1\le\int_{\mathbb T}\Phi_\delta(t)R(t)\,dt.\tag{1}\]

A Riesz-product proof chooses \(R\) so that its linear Fourier contribution is large while terms arising from additive relations are small or cancel. This note isolates a finite cancellation mechanism.

2. Relation hypergraphs

Let \(M\subset\mathbb Z\) be selected Fourier frequencies. A typical unwanted term is indexed by

\[\sum_{m\in M}\sigma_m m=jv,\qquad\sigma_m\in\{-1,0,1\},\tag{2}\]

with \(v\in V\) and controlled quotient \(j\). Its support is \(S=\{m:\sigma_m\ne0\}\). Make the relevant supports hyperedges on vertex set \(M\).

Odd coloring

A coloring is odd on a support \(S\) when some color occurs an odd number of times in \(S\), equivalently when its parity vector is nonzero.

Odd coloring is established hypergraph terminology. Its role here is different: it is the exact combinatorial condition detected by the correlated amplitude law below. A rainbow coloring is sufficient but unnecessarily strong because it distinguishes every pair rather than only the parity information seen by the moment.

3. The two-color amplitude law

Let \((Z_0,Z_1)\in\{-1,1\}^2\) have distribution

ValueProbability
\((1,1)\)\(1/2\)
\((1,-1)\)\(1/4\)
\((-1,1)\)\(1/4\)

Direct calculation gives

\[\mathbb EZ_0=\mathbb EZ_1=\frac12,\qquad\mathbb E[Z_0Z_1]=0.\tag{3}\]
Theorem 3.1 — exact count criterion

If \(m,n\in\mathbb N\) and \(m+n\) is even, then

\[\mathbb E[Z_0^mZ_1^n]=0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad m\text{ is odd or }n\text{ is odd}.\tag{4}\]

If neither exponent is odd, both are even and the expectation is exactly one.

Because \(m+n\) is even, the exponents have the same parity. If both are odd, odd powers of signs equal the signs and the expectation is \(\mathbb E[Z_0Z_1]=0\). If both are even, each power equals one pointwise, so the product is identically one. □

Corollary 3.2 — exact support criterion

If \(S\) has even size and \(c:S\to\{0,1\}\), then

\[\mathbb E\prod_{x\in S}Z_{c(x)}=0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad c\text{ is odd on }S.\tag{5}\]

The two color multiplicities sum to \(|S|\), so they have the same parity. Apply Theorem 3.1. □

For arbitrary real weights \(a_x\),

\[\mathbb E\sum_xa_xZ_{c(x)}=\frac12\sum_xa_x.\tag{6}\]

Thus the exact cancellation criterion retains a constant fraction of the first-level mass.

Riesz positivity

For \(0\le\rho\le1\), \(|u|\le1\), and real \(\theta\),

\[1-\rho u\cos\theta\ge0.\tag{7}\]

Every finite product of such factors is nonnegative, as required in (1).

4. Odd-moment scalar filters

Odd support sizes can be removed by multiplying every color amplitude by one independent scalar \(T\). A support of size \(k\) then gains the factor \(\mathbb E[T^k]\).

Through level four

Let \(T_3=1/2\) with probability \(8/9\), and \(T_3=-1\) with probability \(1/9\). Then

\[|T_3|\le1,\quad\mathbb ET_3=\frac13,\quad\mathbb E[T_3^3]=0.\tag{8}\]

The mean is sharp under \(T\ge-1\) and \(\mathbb E[T^3]=0\), since \((T+1)(2T-1)^2=1+4T^3-3T\ge0\).

Through level six

Let \(s=\sqrt5\), and let \(T_5\) take values \(1,(s-1)/4,-(s+1)/4\) with probabilities \(1/25,4(3+s)/25,4(3-s)/25\). Then

\[|T_5|\le1,\quad\mathbb ET_5=\frac15,\quad\mathbb E[T_5^3]=\mathbb E[T_5^5]=0.\tag{9}\]

The mean is sharp under \(T\le1\) and these two zero-moment conditions, since \((1-T)(4T^2+2T-1)^2=1+20T^3-16T^5-5T\ge0\).

The Chebyshev-type scalar extremality is not itself the claimed novelty. Its purpose here is to factor the cancellation problem by parity: the scalar law sees odd support sizes, while the colored sign law sees even support sizes.

5. Finite cancellation interface

Let one Boolean coloring be odd on every designated even support. With \(Z_0,Z_1\) as above and independent scalar \(T\), put \(U_m=T Z_{c(m)}\). For a support \(S\),

\[\mathbb E\prod_{m\in S}U_m=\mathbb E[T^{|S|}]\;\mathbb E\prod_{m\in S}Z_{c(m)}.\tag{10}\]
Theorem 5.1 — exact low-level cancellation

Using \(T_3\), all designated terms of support size \(2,3,4\) vanish. Using \(T_5\), all designated terms of support size \(2,3,4,5,6\) vanish.

Even sizes vanish by Corollary 3.2; sizes \(3\), or \(3\) and \(5\), vanish by the scalar moments. □

The surviving first moments are \(\mathbb EU_m=1/6\) for \(T_3\), and \(1/10\) for \(T_5\).

6. Conditional analytic consequence

Not part of the Lean-certified theorem. This section records the analytic interface needed for an asymptotic improvement.

Existing large-dimension Riesz methods select positive first-level mass \(A\ge d/n^{1+o(1)}\), where \(d\) is an additive dimension. Suppose additionally that all even relation supports below a cutoff admit one odd two-coloring, and that after exact cancellation the quotient and high-level tails are \(o(\rho A)\) for some \(\rho=n^{-o(1)}\). Then the Riesz inequality would give

\[\operatorname{ML}(V)-\frac1{2n}\ge\frac{d}{n^{2+o(1)}}.\tag{11}\]

Balancing (11) with the existing small-dimension scale \(1/(n^{1+o(1)}d)\) yields the conditional target

\[\operatorname{ML}(V)\ge\frac1{2n}+\frac1{n^{3/2+o(1)}}.\tag{12}\]

Equation (12) is not claimed as a theorem in this repository.

7. Parity-circuit obstruction

Write a Boolean coloring as variables \(x_m\in\mathbb F_2\). For every even hyperedge \(S\), odd coloring is

\[\sum_{m\in S}x_m=1\pmod2.\tag{13}\]

The system is inconsistent exactly when an odd family of hyperedges has empty symmetric difference: every active frequency occurs an even number of times across an odd number of relation supports. This is the dual certificate for failure of the cancellation construction.

Next arithmetic problem

For the short-relation hypergraphs arising from dissociated frequencies, either solve (13), or turn an odd parity circuit into coherent modular or divisibility structure that directly produces a lonely time.

9. Prior work and precise novelty claim

What is not claimed as new

Odd coloring and odd chromatic number were introduced in hypergraph coloring before this work. Riesz products and dissociated sets are classical, and recent Lonely Runner arguments already use Riesz products, Fourier energy, and phase randomization. The use of polynomial inequalities to solve finite moment extremal problems is also classical. A 2026 function-field analogue explicitly suggests searching for correlated phase laws whose Fourier transform vanishes on harmful relation classes.

What this work adds

The new object is the following interface:

\[\boxed{\text{Lonely Runner relation-support hypergraph}\ +\ \text{odd coloring}\ \Longleftrightarrow\ \text{exact correlated-amplitude cancellation}}\]

More precisely, the contribution consists of four linked steps:

  1. Support-level encoding. Harmful low-order signed relations are organized as hyperedges by their supports, rather than being controlled only through a pair-conflict graph or aggregate quadratic energy.
  2. Exact detector law. The explicit positive-mean three-atom law satisfies the if-and-only-if criterion (5): on an even support, odd coloring is exactly the zero-moment condition, while failure gives moment one.
  3. Parity factorization. An independent scalar filter kills designated odd support sizes, leaving the colored law to handle even sizes without losing boundedness or Riesz positivity.
  4. Dual obstruction. Failure of odd two-colorability has the exact \(\mathbb F_2\) certificate described in Section 7, converting an analytic obstruction into a finite parity circuit among labelled arithmetic relations.

These statements together are stronger and more precise than saying that “random phases reduce the quadratic error.” They identify the exact finite combinatorial property required by this amplitude law and the exact structure produced when that property fails.

Priority status

A targeted search of arXiv, published references, and public code through 31 August 2026 found no earlier Lonely Runner source combining all four steps above. Bedert and Hu obtain the numerical \(13/8\) secondary exponent by a different phase-randomized quadratic-energy method; this exponent is not claimed as new here. Hu's function-field paper contains the broad correlated-phase direction, but not this explicit law, the odd-color equivalence, the parity factorization, or the Lean formalization.

Qualification. Public search cannot prove absolute historical priority. The defensible claim is that this exact synthesis and exact equivalence were not located in the public literature searched. Unpublished notes, private communication, or an equivalent construction under different language may exist.
