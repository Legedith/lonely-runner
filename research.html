Odd-Color Amplitude Filters for Lonely Runner Riesz Products

A finite cancellation mechanism, a machine-checked proof, and a precise arithmetic problem left for the analytic method.

Jatin Dehmiwal · AI-assisted exploration and formal proof engineering

Abstract

Riesz-product arguments for the Lonely Runner Conjecture gain from selected positive Fourier coefficients but pay for low-order additive relations. We introduce a finite probability law that converts an odd two-coloring of even relation supports into exact moment cancellation. The law has common coordinate mean \(1/2\), so it retains a constant fraction of first-order gain. Independent scalar filters annihilate the third moment, or the third and fifth moments, and are sharp under their stated constraints. We formalize the finite algebra, parity step, scalar filters, and positivity of the Riesz factors in Lean 4. The full conjecture is not proved: the remaining problem is to control the odd chromatic structure of the actual arithmetic relation hypergraph and complete the analytic transfer.

1. Lonely Runner and Riesz products

For distinct positive integers \(V=\{v_1,\ldots,v_n\}\), define

\[\operatorname{ML}(V)=\max_{t\in\mathbb R/\mathbb Z}\min_{v\in V}\|tv\|,\]

where \(\|x\|\) is distance to the nearest integer. The stationary-runner form of the Lonely Runner Conjecture is

Lonely Runner Conjecture
\[\operatorname{ML}(V)\ge\frac1{n+1}.\]

For \(0<\delta<1/2\), let

\[\Phi_\delta(t)=\sum_{v\in V}\mathbf1_{\{\|vt\|\le\delta\}}.\]

If the bad arcs cover the circle, then \(\Phi_\delta\ge1\). Every nonnegative density \(R\) of integral one therefore satisfies

\[1\le\int_{\mathbb T}\Phi_\delta(t)R(t)\,dt.\tag{1}\]

A Riesz-product proof chooses \(R\) so that its linear Fourier contribution is large while terms arising from additive relations are small or cancel. This note isolates a finite cancellation mechanism.

2. Relation hypergraphs

Let \(M\subset\mathbb Z\) be selected Fourier frequencies. A typical unwanted term is indexed by

\[\sum_{m\in M}\sigma_m m=jv,\qquad\sigma_m\in\{-1,0,1\},\tag{2}\]

with \(v\in V\) and controlled quotient \(j\). Its support is \(S=\{m:\sigma_m\ne0\}\). Make the relevant supports hyperedges on vertex set \(M\).

Odd coloring

A coloring is odd on a support \(S\) when some color occurs an odd number of times in \(S\), equivalently when its parity vector is nonzero.

Odd coloring is known hypergraph terminology. Its role here is new: it is exactly the combinatorial condition seen by the correlated amplitude law below. A rainbow coloring is sufficient but unnecessarily strong.

3. The two-color amplitude law

Let \((Z_0,Z_1)\in\{-1,1\}^2\) have distribution

ValueProbability
\((1,1)\)\(1/2\)
\((1,-1)\)\(1/4\)
\((-1,1)\)\(1/4\)

Direct calculation gives

\[\mathbb EZ_0=\mathbb EZ_1=\frac12,\qquad\mathbb E[Z_0Z_1]=0.\tag{3}\]
Theorem 3.1 — even-support cancellation

If \(m,n\in\mathbb N\), \(m+n\) is even, and one of \(m,n\) is odd, then

\[\mathbb E[Z_0^mZ_1^n]=0.\tag{4}\]

Both exponents are odd. Odd powers of signs equal the signs, so the expectation is \(\mathbb E[Z_0Z_1]=0\). □

Corollary 3.2 — odd-colored support

If \(S\) has even size and \(c:S\to\{0,1\}\) is odd on \(S\), then

\[\mathbb E\prod_{x\in S}Z_{c(x)}=0.\tag{5}\]

The two color multiplicities sum to \(|S|\). At least one is odd; because the sum is even, both are odd. Apply Theorem 3.1. □

For arbitrary real weights \(a_x\),

\[\mathbb E\sum_xa_xZ_{c(x)}=\frac12\sum_xa_x.\tag{6}\]

Thus cancellation retains a constant fraction of the first-level mass.

Riesz positivity

For \(0\le\rho\le1\), \(|u|\le1\), and real \(\theta\),

\[1-\rho u\cos\theta\ge0.\tag{7}\]

Every finite product of such factors is nonnegative, as required in (1).

4. Odd-moment scalar filters

Odd support sizes can be removed by multiplying every color amplitude by one independent scalar \(T\). A support of size \(k\) then gains the factor \(\mathbb E[T^k]\).

Through level four

Let \(T_3=1/2\) with probability \(8/9\), and \(T_3=-1\) with probability \(1/9\). Then

\[|T_3|\le1,\quad\mathbb ET_3=\frac13,\quad\mathbb E[T_3^3]=0.\tag{8}\]

The mean is sharp under \(T\ge-1\) and \(\mathbb E[T^3]=0\), since \((T+1)(2T-1)^2=1+4T^3-3T\ge0\).

Through level six

Let \(s=\sqrt5\), and let \(T_5\) take values \(1,(s-1)/4,-(s+1)/4\) with probabilities \(1/25,4(3+s)/25,4(3-s)/25\). Then

\[|T_5|\le1,\quad\mathbb ET_5=\frac15,\quad\mathbb E[T_5^3]=\mathbb E[T_5^5]=0.\tag{9}\]

The mean is sharp under \(T\le1\) and these two zero-moment conditions, since \((1-T)(4T^2+2T-1)^2=1+20T^3-16T^5-5T\ge0\).

5. Finite cancellation interface

Let one Boolean coloring be odd on every designated even support. With \(Z_0,Z_1\) as above and independent scalar \(T\), put \(U_m=T Z_{c(m)}\). For a support \(S\),

\[\mathbb E\prod_{m\in S}U_m=\mathbb E[T^{|S|}]\;\mathbb E\prod_{m\in S}Z_{c(m)}.\tag{10}\]
Theorem 5.1 — exact low-level cancellation

Using \(T_3\), all designated terms of support size \(2,3,4\) vanish. Using \(T_5\), all designated terms of support size \(2,3,4,5,6\) vanish.

Even sizes vanish by Corollary 3.2; sizes \(3\), or \(3\) and \(5\), vanish by the scalar moments. □

The surviving first moments are \(\mathbb EU_m=1/6\) for \(T_3\), and \(1/10\) for \(T_5\).

6. Conditional analytic consequence

Not part of the Lean-certified theorem. This section records the analytic interface needed for an asymptotic improvement.

Existing large-dimension Riesz methods select positive first-level mass \(A\ge d/n^{1+o(1)}\), where \(d\) is an additive dimension. Suppose additionally that all even relation supports below a cutoff admit one odd two-coloring, and that after exact cancellation the quotient and high-level tails are \(o(\rho A)\) for some \(\rho=n^{-o(1)}\). Then the Riesz inequality would give

\[\operatorname{ML}(V)-\frac1{2n}\ge\frac{d}{n^{2+o(1)}}.\tag{11}\]

Balancing (11) with the existing small-dimension scale \(1/(n^{1+o(1)}d)\) yields the conditional target

\[\operatorname{ML}(V)\ge\frac1{2n}+\frac1{n^{3/2+o(1)}}.\tag{12}\]

Equation (12) is not claimed as a theorem in this repository.

7. Parity-circuit obstruction

Write a Boolean coloring as variables \(x_m\in\mathbb F_2\). For every even hyperedge \(S\), odd coloring is

\[\sum_{m\in S}x_m=1\pmod2.\tag{13}\]

The system is inconsistent exactly when an odd family of hyperedges has empty symmetric difference: every active frequency occurs an even number of times across an odd number of relation supports.

Next arithmetic problem

For the short-relation hypergraphs arising from dissociated frequencies, either solve (13), or turn an odd parity circuit into coherent modular or divisibility structure that directly produces a lonely time.

9. Prior work and novelty status

Odd coloring and parity vectors are established concepts in hypergraph coloring. Riesz products, dissociated sets, and phase randomization already appear in recent Lonely Runner work. A recent function-field analogue also suggests correlated phase laws vanishing on harmful relation classes.

The contribution isolated here is the synthesis

\[\text{odd relation-support coloring}+\text{explicit correlated two-color law}+\text{odd-moment filter}.\]

A targeted search of arXiv, published references, and public code through 31 August 2026 found no earlier Lonely Runner construction using this exact interface. This supports a provisional novelty claim but cannot exclude unpublished or differently phrased equivalent work.

The numerical \(13/8\) secondary exponent is not new here: Bedert and Hu already obtain it by a different phase-randomized quadratic-energy method. This note supplies a different finite mechanism and a parity-circuit problem that could move the method toward the \(3/2\) scale.
