Odd-color amplitude filters for Lonely Runner Riesz products

We introduce a new interface for Riesz-product attacks on the Lonely Runner Conjecture. Harmful additive-relation supports are treated as hyperedges, and an explicit correlated sign law has zero mixed moment exactly when an even support is odd-colored. The law keeps coordinate mean \(1/2\), while independent sharp scalar filters remove odd support sizes. Lean 4 checks this finite mechanism.

The central theorem

Odd coloring is the exact condition detected by the law

Let \((Z_0,Z_1)\) take the following three values. This is a correlated probability law, not an independence assumption.

OutcomeProbability
\((1,1)\)\(1/2\)
\((1,-1)\)\(1/4\)
\((-1,1)\)\(1/4\)
Exact finite cancellation theorem

Let \(S\) be an even finite support and let \(c:S\to\{0,1\}\). Then

\[\mathbb E\prod_{x\in S} Z_{c(x)}=0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\text{some color occurs oddly many times in }S.\]

Also \(\mathbb EZ_0=\mathbb EZ_1=1/2\). The law therefore keeps half of the weighted first-level mass while cancelling precisely the odd-colored even supports.

On an even support, the two color counts have the same parity. If they are both odd, the product reduces to \(Z_0Z_1\), whose expectation is zero. If they are both even, every sign is raised to an even power, so the product is identically one. □

What is genuinely new

The ingredients existed; this exact interface did not appear in the public LRC literature

The defensible novelty claim is architectural. Riesz products, phase randomization, odd hypergraph coloring, and Chebyshev polynomials are established ideas. This work connects them in a way that exposes a new finite invariant and a new obstruction for the Lonely Runner problem.

1

Relation-support hypergraph

Low-order Fourier errors are organized by the supports of signed additive relations. The relevant complexity is the odd-color structure of this hypergraph, not merely pairwise conflicts, rainbow coloring, or aggregate quadratic energy.

2

Exact moment detector

The three-atom law does not merely give a sufficient cancellation trick. On every even support, zero moment is equivalent to odd coloring; failure of odd coloring forces moment exactly one. This equivalence is machine checked.

3

Parity factorization

The colored sign law handles even support sizes. An independent scalar moment filter handles odd support sizes. The two tasks separate cleanly while preserving bounded amplitudes, positive first-level gain, and nonnegative Riesz factors.

4

Exact failure certificate

For two colors, the coloring conditions form a linear system over \(\mathbb F_2\). Inconsistency is equivalent to an odd family of relation supports with empty symmetric difference—a concrete parity circuit that arithmetic methods can attack.

Priority statement. Public Lonely Runner Riesz-product work before this used dissociation, Fourier-energy estimates, and phase randomization. Odd coloring was already known in hypergraph theory, and the 2026 function-field analogue suggested correlated phase laws in general terms. A targeted search through 31 August 2026 found no earlier public source combining a Lonely Runner relation-support hypergraph, the exact odd-color/zero-moment equivalence, independent odd-moment filters, and the parity-circuit obstruction. This is evidence for novelty, not a claim that unpublished equivalent work is impossible.

Closest public sources: Bedert's Riesz-product method, Bedert–Hu phase randomization, the original hypergraph odd-coloring definition, and Hu's function-field correlated-phase proposal.

Why odd coloring appears

Relations become hyperedges

In a Riesz-product expansion, each harmful low-order additive relation has a finite support. Regard supports as hyperedges and color their frequencies with two colors. The exact theorem above then turns the analytic requirement “make this Fourier term vanish” into the combinatorial requirement “odd-color this hyperedge.”

1

Encode

Vertices are selected Fourier frequencies. Hyperedges are supports of the short relations producing unwanted terms.

2

Odd-color

Every designated even hyperedge must contain a color of odd multiplicity.

3

Cancel or certify

A solution cancels every designated even term. Failure yields an explicit \(\mathbb F_2\) parity circuit rather than an unstructured analytic loss.

Boundary. The formal theorem proves this finite cancellation interface. It does not prove that every Lonely Runner relation hypergraph has a suitable odd coloring, nor does it formalize all Fourier-analytic tail estimates.
Formal verification

What the kernel checks

Exact equivalence

twoColor_oddSupport_iff_moment_zero proves that, for even supports, odd coloring is equivalent to zero mixed moment.

Two-color law

Normalization, nonnegative weights, sign bounds, common mean \(1/2\), zero odd–odd moment, and unit even–even moment.

Scalar filters

Explicit filters of mean \(1/3\) and \(1/5\) kill the third, or third and fifth, moments. Matching sharp upper bounds are proved.

Riesz positivity

For \(0\le\rho\le1\) and \(|u|\le1\), each factor \(1-\rho u\cos\theta\) and every finite product is nonnegative.
